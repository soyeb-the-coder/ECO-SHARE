Act I — The Reality We Ignore

In one kitchen, food is thrown into dustbins.
Outside, a child hasn't eaten since yesterday.

India wastes 67 million tonnes of food every year — enough to feed every malnourished person in the country three times over. While canteens dump full plates, daily wage labourers, homeless families, and orphan children search through garbage for their next meal. This isn't a distant problem — it's happening right outside your campus gate.

Act II — The Invisible Climate Cost

That rotting food in landfills doesn't just stink...
it's heating our planet faster than cars.

When food decomposes without oxygen in landfills, it releases methane — a greenhouse gas 25× more potent than CO₂. India's food waste alone generates more methane than the entire aviation industry. And while this gas is destroying our atmosphere, we're burning fossil fuels for the same energy methane could have given us.

Act III — The Bridge

What if surplus food reached hungry hands
before it touched a dustbin?

EcoShare connects campus mess halls, event organizers, and canteens directly with local volunteers, slum communities, and orphanages. Fresh food that would be dumped reaches someone who hasn't eaten today — in under 30 minutes.

But the story doesn't end here...

Not all leftover food is safe for humans

Burnt rice, stale bread, vegetable peels, overripe fruits — what canteens throw away as "waste" is actually food that 60 million stray animals are starving for. And the food even they can't eat? That still has power left in it.

Act IV — They're Starving Too

A stray dog eats from a garbage truck...
while a canteen throws away 50 kg of rice.

Walk outside any campus and you'll see them — rib-showing stray dogs, blind calves tied to poles, injured cats hiding under parked cars. India has 60 million stray animals, most surviving on nothing but dirty water and plastic. Meanwhile, canteens throw away vegetable peels, stale rotis, leftover rice, and overripe fruits — food that could keep these animals alive for another day.

EcoShare routes this rejected food to local shelters, street dog feeders, and Gaushalas — turning what canteens consider "waste" into life-saving meals. Every batch saved from the dustbin means another animal eats tonight.

Act V — Waste to Wheels

The leftover that even animals can't eat...
can fuel your car tomorrow.

The truly rotten, spoiled, inedible food waste doesn't go to landfill — it goes into biogas digesters. Through anaerobic decomposition, bacteria break it down into methane gas. When purified, this becomes biomethane — chemically identical to CNG. India already runs buses on biogas. With enough campus food waste, we can power auto-rickshaws, bikes, and generators right from our canteens.

🚗

How Biogas Works

1

Food waste is collected from canteens and events — anything too spoiled for animals

2

It goes into an anaerobic digester — a sealed tank where bacteria break it down without oxygen

3

The bacteria produce methane (CH₄) — the same gas in LPG cylinders

4

Methane is purified and stored — removing CO₂ and impurities

5

Clean biomethane powers CNG vehicles, cooking stoves, and generators — zero fossil fuel needed

6

100 kg food waste = 150 km car travel — waste that would pollute landfills now runs your commute
